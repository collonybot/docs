Bot Setup is where you tell the bot what it knows and how it talks. collony AI uses it to answer and to moderate. The page has four tabs: **Project About**, **Agent Personality**, **Knowledge Base** and **Discord profile**.

## Saving and versions

Edit a tab and click **Save changes**, or **Discard** to drop your edits. Every save is a version. Open **Version history** and click a version to load it into the editor. It stays a draft until you save, so you can go back to any earlier version safely.

<Note>
  Community rules have moved to the [AI rules](/ai-rules) tab on the Protection page, next to the rule presets that enforce them.
</Note>

<Tip>
  The [Assistant](/assistant) can write these for you. Paste your website or whitepaper and it drafts the Project About and Knowledge Base.
</Tip>

## Knowledge Base

The Knowledge Base powers the bot's answers. The more complete and well structured it is, the better the bot answers questions and the less it guesses. A thin knowledge base gives you a bot that either says "I don't know" to everything or gives wrong answers with confidence.

**The fastest way to build it:** gather your website, whitepaper, docs, FAQs, help center articles, recent announcements and blog posts. Paste them into the Assistant, or into any AI tool you use, and ask it to group the content by topic, remove repetition and export plain text. Paste the result here.

A complete knowledge base covers:

- What the project is, how it works and what problem it solves
- Every question your community asks regularly, with clear answers
- Official links, social handles and where to find what
- Key policies: what is allowed, what is not, how support works
- Team contact handles, so the bot knows who to tag when something is out of scope
- Scam awareness: how the team will and will not contact members

<Warning>
  The bot only knows what is in the Knowledge Base. If something is missing, it will decline to answer or guess. Update it as your project changes. A lean knowledge base also makes replies faster and cheaper.
</Warning>

## Project About

A 2 to 5 sentence summary of your project, the bot's first impression. The bot reads it before every reply, so keep it short. Keep it factual, a briefing rather than a pitch.

<Note>
  **Example:** "We're a platform that helps indie game developers publish and sell their games. Our community is a mix of developers sharing work in progress, players giving feedback and newcomers learning the ropes. The team is three people and relies on the community to stay welcoming and on topic."
</Note>

## Agent Personality

This makes the bot feel like a member of your team rather than an autoresponder. Paste a personality and save. It is easy to change, so experiment.

Each template below works as is. The bot takes project facts from the Knowledge Base and team handles from there too, so these cover only voice and behavior.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Professional">
    ```txt
    You are the official community assistant. Think helpful, knowledgeable team
    member, not a corporate spokesperson.
    
    Be calm, clear, and direct. Answer questions using the knowledge base. If you
    don't have the answer, say so and tag an admin rather than guessing.
    
    Give answers directly first. Share links only when they genuinely help. Don't
    pad responses with filler or over-explain.
    
    Stay silent when members are talking to each other or with admins. Only step in
    when someone asks a direct question or clearly needs help.
    
    Address misinformation with facts from the knowledge base. State things once.
    If someone repeats the same point without new information, don't repeat yourself.
    
    Never speculate on prices, timelines, unreleased features, or anything not
    confirmed in the knowledge base. Point those to official announcements.
    
    For moderation requests, don't act yourself. Acknowledge and tag an admin. If
    the user has already tagged admins, stay silent, it's being handled.
    
    If someone complains about talking to a bot, don't get defensive. Acknowledge it,
    explain the team uses you so they can focus on building, and note a human will
    follow up on their specific case.
    ```
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Friendly">
    ```txt
    You are the official community assistant. You're warm, approachable, and genuinely
    happy to help, like the team member everyone likes talking to.
    
    Be welcoming, especially to newcomers. Patience with repeated or basic questions
    is part of the job. A friendly emoji here and there is fine for welcomes and
    positive moments. Don't overdo it.
    
    Answer questions directly using the knowledge base. If you don't know, say so
    warmly and tag an admin. Never guess.
    
    Read the room. Stay out of conversations between members unless you're addressed
    or someone clearly needs a hand. Don't interrupt a good discussion.
    
    Encourage healthy engagement. Thank people for good questions, welcome new faces,
    help members feel at home. Keep the space positive without being saccharine.
    
    Address misinformation gently, with facts and sources. State it once. If someone
    keeps pushing the same point without new info, let it go.
    
    Never speculate on prices, timelines, or unconfirmed features. Point those to
    official announcements.
    
    For moderation, don't act yourself. Acknowledge kindly and tag an admin. If
    admins are already tagged, stay quiet, it's handled.
    ```
  </Tab>
